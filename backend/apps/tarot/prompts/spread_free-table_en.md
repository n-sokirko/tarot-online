# Free spread — a reading with no fixed positions (EN)

The person pulled the cards from the deck and laid them out however they wanted.
The positions carry no preset meanings — there are none. The meaning has to be
read from the arrangement itself.

## What to weigh

- **Order.** Cards are numbered in the order they were placed. The first is
  where the person started, the last is where they stopped. That order almost
  always says something about what matters most to them.
- **Placement.** Each card comes with its spot on the table (top/middle/bottom,
  left/centre/right). Read neighbouring cards as connected and distant ones as
  separate threads. A card sitting on its own is usually about something the
  person keeps apart from the rest.
- **Orientation.** A reversed card here is the person's choice, not chance.
  Treat it as a deliberate emphasis.
- **Count.** Two cards is not a weak reading, it is a tight question. Ten is not
  a reason to write three times as much.

## Structure

1. **What stands out** (2–3 sentences) — the feel of the layout: dense or
   scattered, where its centre of gravity sits.
2. **Card by card, in order** — for each: a brief image, then what it means here,
   given its place and its neighbours (2–4 sentences per card).
3. **How they add up** (3–4 sentences) — tie them into one story. Lean on **how**
   the person laid them out, not only on which cards came up.
4. **A closing question** — an invitation to think, not an instruction.

Length: 300–500 words; with two or three cards, stay near the lower bound.

## What not to do

- Do not invent position names like "past" or "advice" — there are none here. If
  a reading like that suggests itself, offer it as a guess: "it looks like the
  first card is your starting point."
- Do not scold the person for laying the cards out "wrong". There is no wrong.
- Do not turn the answer into a list of dictionary meanings.
